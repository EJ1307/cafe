01Our story

su·se·gad

/soo-seh-gaad/ adj. Konkani

  1. Content, unhurried, at ease.
  2. The Goan art of the slow afternoon: a long lunch, a short nap, a chair on the balcão.
  3. How you’ll feel after the second cup.

See also balcão, siesta, “five more minutes”.

A bit of Fontainhas on Greene Avenue

Anika Fernandes grew up two lanes from 31st January Road in Panjim, in a house with ochre walls, cobalt shutters and a tiled bench by the door where the whole street seemed to sit in the afternoons.

Joel Mathias spent ten years roasting coffee in Bengaluru. In 2016 they took over an old locksmith’s on Greene Avenue, painted the front the exact ochre of her grandmother’s house in Fontainhas, hung blue shutters and built a tiled bench out front. In Goa it’s called a balcão. In Fort Greene, people just sit on it.

The back room became the roastery. The kitchen became a bakery, because a morning without poee is a morning that hasn’t started, in Panjim or in Brooklyn. The rest of the time, we mostly try not to hurry anyone.

— Anika & Joel

03The pour

Three hundred pours a day, no two alike

Every milk drink leaves the lever machine with something drawn on it. The milk is steamed to about 140°F — sweet, glossy, drinkable straight away — and poured into a double shot from a height, then very close. If yours comes out a little wobbly, it is still on the menu, and it tastes exactly the same.

05Bakery & roastery

From the oven at 6 am

Everything on the counter started somewhere earlier and further away. Here is the long way round to your cup and your bun.

  1. 01Twice a year, after harvest

    Source

    Two family estates near Chikmagalur, 3,600–4,600 feet up, coffee growing under pepper vines and silver oak. Joel flies out after every harvest, tastes everything, and we import the green beans we’d drink ourselves.

  2. 02Tuesdays & Fridays, 5 am

    Roast

    Joel roasts in the back room on a five-kilo drum roaster, in batches small enough to taste every one. Espresso goes a shade darker; filter stays light enough to tell you where it grew.

  3. 03Every morning, 4:30 am

    Bake

    The ovens go on in the dark: poee and croissants first, then buns and bolinhas. The first tray is out by six. Bebinca, all seven layers of it, happens on Thursdays.

  4. 04Tue–Sun, from 7 am

    Brew

    Around three hundred milk drinks a day on the lever machine, kaapi decoction dripped overnight in steel, cold brew steeped for twelve hours. Then somebody rolls up the shutters and puts the cat on the bench.
